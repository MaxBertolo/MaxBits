Hush Security says the AI security problem has shifted from protecting models to governing identities as autonomous agents spread

VentureBeat – AI · Topic: AI/Cloud/Quantum

  • What it is: Israeli cybersecurity startup Hush Security announced a $30 million Series A, stating that enterprise AI security has fundamentally shifted from protecting models to governing autonomous agent identities.
  • Who: Hush Security, backed by investors like Battery Ventures, YL Ventures, and strategic investor Akamai, focuses on non-human identity security for software and now autonomous AI agents.
  • What it does: Hush extends its platform into an "Identity Gateway" for AI agents, discovering them, assigning identities, brokering task-specific permissions, and maintaining centralized audit logs.
  • Why it matters: The rapid spread of autonomous AI agents acting independently across multiple enterprise systems creates significant security gaps with existing identity governance models, amplifying risk.
  • Strategic view: TMT executives must urgently implement robust, dedicated identity governance for autonomous AI agents to safely scale AI adoption while mitigating escalating security and compliance risks.

Mastercard spent decades training its fraud system to see bots as thieves. Now bots are the ones doing the buying.

VentureBeat – AI · Topic: AI/Cloud/Quantum

  • What it is: Mastercard is fundamentally evolving its payment fraud systems to enable and secure legitimate transactions conducted by AI agents, shifting from blocking bots to fostering trusted agentic commerce.
  • Who: Mastercard, a global payments technology company, is collaborating with tech giants like Microsoft, OpenAI, and Google, while impacting consumers, businesses, and various AI-powered transactional agents.
  • What it does: Mastercard is developing a 'scaled trust infrastructure' with five layers—identity, verifiable intent, controls, execution via Agent Pay, and intelligence—to secure AI-driven, delegated commerce.
  • Why it matters: This transformation is crucial for enabling autonomous B2B procurement and consumer purchases, ensuring trust, accountability, and the continued scaling of AI-powered services across the economy.
  • Strategic view: For TMT executives, this signifies a critical shift toward 'know your agent' and verifiable intent as foundational requirements for future autonomous commerce, making trust infrastructure a competitive imperative.

The lineage behind 69% of open models was never verified. Cisco just fingerprinted almost 900 for free

VentureBeat – AI · Topic: AI/Cloud/Quantum

  • What it is: Cisco's new AI Supply Chain Provenance Explorer is a free public database that verifies the true lineage and security status of nearly 900 open AI models using advanced fingerprinting.
  • Who: Cisco developed the AI Supply Chain Provenance Explorer to address critical security and trust gaps in open AI models widely used by enterprises and hosted on platforms like Hugging Face.
  • What it does: It accurately fingerprints the derivation of open AI models, replacing unreliable self-reported tags with verified data on lineage, license, and scan coverage to enhance AI supply chain transparency.
  • Why it matters: This matters because unverified AI model lineage poses substantial security risks and compliance challenges, a critical gap traditional supply chain tools fail to adequately address.
  • Strategic view: This tool establishes a vital foundation for secure AI adoption and governance, reducing enterprise risk from unverified models and setting a new standard for AI supply chain transparency.
