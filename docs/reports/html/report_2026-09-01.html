Identity and permissions aren’t enough to govern AI agent behavior

VentureBeat – AI · Topic: AI/Cloud/Quantum

  • What it is: Traditional identity and access management systems are insufficient to secure autonomous AI agents, as they govern what agents can reach, but not how they behave once access is granted.
  • Who: This issue impacts enterprises deploying autonomous AI agents that interact with sensitive data, with insights from Heather Ceylan, Chief Information Security Officer at Box.
  • What it does: Autonomous AI agents can rapidly exploit broad or stale permissions, converting legitimate data access into unintended or harmful actions at machine speed, far beyond human oversight.
  • Why it matters: Legacy security frameworks, designed for human interaction and static permissions, cannot contain the dynamic and rapid execution risks posed by AI agents, leading to critical data breaches and operational failures.
  • Strategic view: C-level executives must transition AI security strategies beyond static access controls to govern agent execution and behavior, integrating dynamic permissions, content classification, and tiered human oversight for high-risk actions.
