Frontier models can recover up to 65% of facts they can't directly recall — just by thinking longer

VentureBeat – AI · Topic: AI/Cloud/Quantum

  • What it is: A new study reveals large language models often encode factual knowledge but struggle with direct recall, which inference-time computation can significantly improve.
  • Who: Researchers from Google Research and Technion conducted experiments on frontier models, demonstrating that inaccessible knowledge is frequently encoded within LLMs.
  • What it does: Inference-time computation, like Chain-of-Thought, acts as a recovery mechanism, enabling models to retrieve 40-65% of facts previously inaccessible due to recall failures.

  • Strategic view: TMT executives must recognize that optimizing existing model knowledge through strategic inference-time interventions offers a more cost-effective path to reliable AI than perpetual scaling or RAG.
